# Working Async

Arrow contributors are spread across the globe, and there is no hour of the day when everyone is awake at once. Async is not a compromise we tolerate, it's the default we design for. The community's rhythm is bursts of focused work at unpredictable times, stitched together by good writing.

[How We Work](/docs/contributing/how-we-work) covers the philosophy. This page is the practical side: the habits that make async collaboration actually work, and what to do when you're blocked at 2am your time and everyone else is asleep.

## Write for the person who wasn't there

Every update you post will be read by someone who missed the conversation that led to it. Write for them.

- **Lead with the state of things.** "The bounty sync now opens a PR instead of pushing to staging" tells a reader everything before they decide whether to keep reading. "Quick update on the thing we discussed" tells them nothing.
- **Link the context instead of assuming it.** Reference the issue, the PR, the Forum thread. A sentence plus three links beats three paragraphs of recap.
- **Record decisions where they'll be found.** A decision that only exists in a Discord scroll is a decision the next contributor will unknowingly make all over again. If it matters next month, it belongs in GitHub, the Forum, or these docs.

## Never block on a response

Waiting is the biggest hidden cost in async work, and most of it is avoidable.

- **Ask early, not when you're stuck.** If you can see a question coming two days out, ask it now and keep building. By the time you need the answer, it's usually waiting for you.
- **Ask so that "yes" is enough.** "I plan to move the discipline list into disciplines.json and point the form at it, any objection?" can be answered in one word from a phone. "How should we handle the discipline list?" starts a meeting.
- **Have a second thread going.** When one piece of work is waiting on someone, switch to the other one. Contributors who always have two things in flight rarely lose a day to a time zone.

## Hand off cleanly

Stepping away is normal. Disappearing mid-task without a note is what hurts.

When you pause a piece of work, leave a comment on the issue or PR saying where it stands, what's left, and anything surprising you learned. Two sentences is usually enough, and it turns an abandoned branch into something the next person can pick up in minutes.

## Time zone etiquette

- Post times in UTC, or use Discord timestamps that render in everyone's local time.
- Expect answers on a scale of hours, not minutes, and don't read silence as disagreement. Silence usually means asleep.
- If something is genuinely urgent, say so explicitly and say why. Urgency loses its meaning when everything is pinged.
- Nobody is expected to be online at any particular hour. Recordings, notes, and written decisions exist precisely so that missing a call costs you nothing.

## When real-time is worth it

Some things are cheaper live: design reviews, untangling a misunderstanding, meeting the people behind the handles. That's what the [community calls](/docs/community/community-calls) are for. Use calls to explore and decide direction, then write the outcome down. The call is for the conversation, the write-up is for the record.
